> [!question]- 1.) Let $G$ be a group and let $H \leq G$. Prove that $Z(G) \cap H \subseteq Z(H)$. 
> Let $z \in Z(G) \cap H$. Then, $zg=gz$ for all $g \in G$. Now, because $H \leq G$ it follows that $zh=hz$ for all $h \in H$ and $z \in Z(H)$. Hence, $Z(G) \cap H \subseteq Z(H)$. 


> [!question]- 2.) Given a group $G$ and an element $g \in G$, prove that $\langle g \rangle$ is a subgroup of $G$ 
> Using the subgroup criterion theorem, it suffices to show that $\langle g \rangle$ is nonempty, closed under the group operation, and closed under inverses. Firstly, the group generated by $g$ is nonempty since it contains $g$. It is closed under the binary operation as for any $a,b \in \mathbb{Z}$ we have that $g^ag^b=g^{a+b}\in \langle g \rangle$. Finally, the group generated by $g$ is closed under inverses as if $g^a \in \langle g\rangle$ for some $a \in \mathbb{Z}$ then $g^{-a}\in \langle g \rangle$ and $g^ag^{-a}=e \in \langle g\rangle$. Thus it is a subgroup of $G$.

> [!question]- 7.) Is the additive group $G = \{ a + b \sqrt{ 2 } : a, b \in \mathbb{Z} \}$ cyclic? 
> Assume for contradiction that $G$ is cyclic, such that there exists some $c, d \in \mathbb{Z}$ so $\langle c + d\sqrt{ 2 }\rangle = G$. Then, because $1 \in G$ we would need $1 = n(c +d\sqrt{ 2 })$ for some $n \in \mathbb{Z}$. This yields $\frac{{1-nc}}{nd}=\sqrt{ 2 }$ but if $d$ is non-zero then this contradicts the fact that $\sqrt{ 2 }$ is irrational, so $d=0$. But, now consider that because $\sqrt{ 2 } \in G$ we need $\sqrt{ 2 }=m(c+d\sqrt{ 2 })$ for some $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. However, with $d = 0$ we get that $\sqrt{ 2 }=mc$, but $\sqrt{ 2 }$ cannot be expressed as the product of two integers, so we reach another contradiction and conclude that $G$ is not cyclic.
