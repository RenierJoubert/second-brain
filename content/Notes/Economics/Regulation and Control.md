Most national economies are dependent on international trade to a specific degree. 

The ratio of imports to exports determines how open or closed an economy is
A relatively open economy is at risk of disruption if its currency fluctuates significantly relative to the countries where it conducts most of it's trade. As the prices it pays or recieves will change. 

If a country is primarily an exporter of goods, then they are inclined to have a weaker currency, as their goods will be more competitively priced, for example China. Whereas, a country that is a net importer (trade deficit) it is incentivized to have a stronger currency as the goods that it imports will cost them less. 

## Cost of borrowing
If a gov runs a budget deficit, and seeks to finance it by borrowing from  investors by issuing bonds, any weakness in currency would deter this investment as they would get paid back and face a loss in terms of their own currency.

If a gov issues bonds in a foreign currency, then any weakness in it's own can dramatically increase the cost it has to pay back

This creates different incenvtives whether to weaken or strengthen their currency. 

to strengthen a currency the government can implement monetary tightening, this involves raising interest rates, reducing the money supply or implementing stricter monetary policy. This all increases the demand for domestic currency.
